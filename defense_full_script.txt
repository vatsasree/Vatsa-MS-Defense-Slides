0 · TITLE — Towards Visually Faithful Document Reconstruction   (~0:45)
============================================================

Good morning, everyone. My name is Sreevatsa.

Today I will present my M.S. thesis, "Towards Visually Faithful Document Reconstruction". My advisor is Dr. Ravi Kiran Sarvadevabhatla.

The idea in one line: we want to turn scanned pages and PDFs into HTML that does not just read like the original — it looks like it.

[pause]

The thesis has three pieces.

FID-HTML — the representation.
REPLICA — the method.
VFDR-Bench — the benchmark and the metrics.

And one thing to keep in mind from the start: this entire talk is itself running in HTML.

1 · HOW THIS TALK IS ORGANISED   (~0:35)
============================================================

The talk has six parts.

First, the problem — and why the appearance of a document matters.

Second, related work — what existing methods keep, and what they lose.

Then my three contributions: FID-HTML, the representation; REPLICA, the system that produces it; and VFDR-Bench, the benchmark that measures it.

And finally, the results, the limitations, and what this work makes possible.

2 · DIVIDER — 01 Introduction   (~0:15)
============================================================

Let us start with the problem.

Reading the words on a page is largely solved. The real question is whether we can keep both what a document says and what it looks like.


============================================================
3 · WHAT IS VISUALLY FAITHFUL DOCUMENT RECONSTRUCTION?   (~2:30)
============================================================

So what do we mean by "visually faithful"?

On the left is an original invoice. On the right, I will rebuild the same document, one layer at a time. This is running live.

Level zero is text only. Every word is there, and in the right order. But it is a wall of text. You cannot tell the heading from the body. The table has become a list of numbers. The page shape does not even match.

[pause]

Look at the red notes on the right. With only the text, a reader could pay a charge that was cancelled, miss an overdue notice, and misread the total.

So the style is not decoration. Style is data.

[CLICK]

Level one adds logical structure. Now we know what is a heading, what is a section, what is the footer, and what is a table with rows and columns.

That is why the first two notes turn green.

It is better. But it is still not the page.

[CLICK]

Level two adds physical position. Now the page has the right size, the two columns come back, and the numbers line up under their headers.

The column note turns green.

[CLICK]

Level three adds visual fidelity. Typography, colour, font weight, the strikethrough on the cancelled line, and the overdue stamp.

Now the right side looks exactly like the left side, and the last three notes turn green.

[pause]

So the point is simple: a document is more than its text.


============================================================
4 · REQUIREMENTS FOR VISUALLY FAITHFUL DOCUMENT RECONSTRUCTION   (~1:00)
============================================================

This gives us the requirements for the problem.

[CLICK]

There are four axes.

Text extraction — are the words right?
Logical structure — is the hierarchy right?
Physical structure — is everything in the right place?
Visual fidelity — does it actually look right?

Most existing work focuses on the first two. The last two were never measured, so they were never optimised.

[CLICK]

There are also two practical requirements on the output.

It should be web-native, so it opens anywhere, in any browser.

And it should be machine-readable, so other models can use its structure directly.

These six requirements define the problem for the rest of the thesis.

5 · DIVIDER — 02 Background & Related Work   (~0:15)
============================================================

Next, related work.

The question here is: what do existing methods keep — and what do they lose?


============================================================
6 · WHERE THE FIELD STANDS   (~1:30)
============================================================

Let us see where existing systems stand. On the left is the input page. On the right, I will show one system at a time, running live.

First, Marker, a strong document-parsing tool. The words survive, but the three columns are flattened into one. The page is lost.

[CLICK]

Next, GPT-5. The text is mostly correct, but the layout drifts — the placement, the fonts, even the number of columns.

[CLICK]

And Qwen3-VL-30B. The same story.

[pause]

These are different architectures and different model sizes, but the failure is the same.

Text is mostly there. Hierarchy is roughly there. Geometry is gone. Visual fidelity is gone.

And that consistency is a clue. Maybe the bottleneck is not the model.


============================================================
7 · A TAXONOMY OF PRIOR WORK   (~2:00)
============================================================

Here is the related work in one slide. There are four families of methods, and for each one, I show what it outputs — because what a method outputs limits what it can preserve.

[CLICK]

First, the foundations: OCR engines, layout detectors and document vision-language models. They give us text and boxes, but not a full page.

[CLICK]

Second, document-to-markup tools, such as Marker, MinerU and Docling. They output Markdown or simple HTML, and they flatten the page into a single stream.

[CLICK]

Third, physical reconstruction and screenshot-to-HTML methods. They try to preserve appearance, but only in narrow domains, such as scientific papers or simple web pages.

[CLICK]

And fourth, the benchmarks. They only score text and structure, and their references are written in Markdown.

[pause]

So the field cannot improve what it does not measure.


============================================================
8 · LIMITATIONS OF EXISTING DOCUMENT REPRESENTATIONS   (~1:45)
============================================================

So why does every system lose the same things?

The problem is often the target representation — the format we ask the model to write. A page carries four kinds of information.

[CLICK]

All four have to pass through the output format. Marker writes Markdown. OCR models write plain text. GPT-5 and Qwen write generic HTML.

[CLICK]

And this is where two of the four are lost.

Markdown has nowhere to put an x-y position. Plain text has nowhere to put colour or font style.

Generic HTML can store these things — but it does not require them. And what is optional usually gets left out.

[pause]

So: what a format does not require, a system does not produce.

That observation led us to FID-HTML.


============================================================
9 · DIVIDER — 03 FID-HTML   (~0:15)
============================================================

This brings me to my first contribution.

If the format is the limit, then we need a better format.


============================================================
10 · THE SAME PAGE, REBUILT   (~0:40)
============================================================

This is the same page you just saw the other systems fail on.

But now the target format carries all four axes: text, hierarchy, geometry and appearance.

[CLICK]

It is a single HTML file, rendered directly by the browser.

We call this format FID-HTML.


============================================================
11 · SIX ASPECTS OF FID-HTML   (~1:45)
============================================================

FID-HTML carries six kinds of information. The code on the right is the actual source of the page on the left — it is not a mock-up.

[CLICK]

One: the textual content.

[CLICK]

Two: the hierarchical structure, through nested elements.

[CLICK]

Three: semantic tags, such as lists, tables, rows and cells.

[CLICK]

Four: position — where every block sits on the page.

[CLICK]

Five: styling — font size, colour, bold, italic, underline and strikethrough.

[CLICK]

And six: figures and backgrounds.

[pause]

So the browser sees HTML, and a language model sees text. Both of them see the same document.


============================================================
12 · COMPARISON WITH EXISTING REPRESENTATIONS   (~1:30)
============================================================

Now let us compare FID-HTML with the existing formats, using the same picture as before.

[CLICK]

Text and hierarchy pass through every format — Markdown, plain text, LaTeX, DocTags, hOCR and generic HTML.

[CLICK]

But geometry and style do not.

Markdown and plain text cannot express them at all. LaTeX, DocTags and hOCR support them only partly. And generic HTML allows styling, but does not require position.

[CLICK]

FID-HTML opens a second path.

[CLICK]

In FID-HTML, geometry and styling are mandatory. Every block has a position, and every piece of text carries its visual style.

So fidelity is no longer something we hope the model remembers. It is part of the format.


============================================================
13 · DIVIDER — 04 REPLICA   (~0:15)
============================================================

That was the format. My second contribution is the system that produces it — REPLICA.


============================================================
14 · DEFINING AGENTS AND TOOLS   (~1:15)
============================================================

First, a quick definition of "agentic".

A tool is deterministic. The same input always gives the same output. For example: OCR, layout detection and font fitting.

An agent makes decisions. It can inspect a result, choose, reject, retry or override.

[CLICK]

The picture shows the difference. A tool makes one pass. An agent checks its result, and if it is not good enough, it tries again.

[pause]

Our design rule is simple: use agents where judgment is needed, and tools where a fixed answer is enough.


============================================================
15 · THE AGENTIC FRAMEWORK, STAGE BY STAGE   (~2:30)
============================================================

REPLICA works in four stages. I will build them up one at a time.

[CLICK]

Stage one: Segment. We find the meaningful parts of the page and build a layout hierarchy.

[CLICK]

Stage two: Localize. Each part is sent to the right specialist — for text, tables, images, forms, lists or equations — and each specialist writes a small piece of HTML.

[CLICK]

Stage three: Assemble. We merge these pieces into one document and work out the reading order.

[CLICK]

Stage four: Refine. We render the HTML, compare it with the original page, fix the styling, and render again.

[pause]

So REPLICA does not generate HTML once. It looks at what it produced, and corrects it.


============================================================
16 · WALKTHROUGH OF THE REPLICA AGENTIC FRAMEWORK   (~3:00)
============================================================
(Press Space to play, or click to step through. The slide moves on after the last step.)

Rather than explain the pipeline again, let me show it running.

This is a Telugu newspaper — a deliberately difficult example.

[animation begins]

First, segmentation proposes regions on the page.

Now watch the grouping agent. It does not treat these as separate boxes. It groups the headline, the image and the body text into articles.

[pause while the animation moves]

Next, localization. Different regions go to different specialists, and each one becomes a small piece of FID-HTML.

[pause]

Then assembly. Newspapers are where reading order becomes hard, because column order is not story order. So the reading-order agent works at the level of articles.

[pause]

And finally refinement: render, compare with the original, correct, and render again.

[PAUSE near the final frame]

So what looks like one big problem is really a sequence of much smaller decisions. That decomposition is the core of REPLICA.

19 · VFDR-BENCH — 5,000 DOCUMENTS   (~0:50)

============================================================
20 · WHICH METRIC MEASURES WHAT   (~1:45)
============================================================

The metrics follow the same four axes.

[CLICK]

For text extraction and logical structure, we use standard metrics: character and word recognition rates for text, and tree-edit distance for the structure.

[CLICK]

For the other two axes, we introduce new metrics.

For physical structure, GPS and LPS measure where each block and each line actually landed on the page.

For visual fidelity, VFS asks a vision-language model to compare how the two pages look.

[pause]

Importantly, these new metrics evaluate the rendered page, not just the HTML code.

[CLICK]

The overall score is the average of the four axes, so a system cannot score well by being good at only one.


============================================================
21 · DO THE NEW METRICS AGREE WITH HUMAN EXPERTS?   (~0:50)
============================================================

Because these metrics are new, we checked whether they agree with people.

Seven experts compared the outputs of five systems on 100 documents. This table shows how well each expert agrees with each metric.

For every expert and every metric, the agreement is above 0.8.

[CLICK]

And the overall score agrees best, at 0.89.

25 · ABLATION STUDIES   (~1:45)
============================================================

The ablation study shows where these gains come from.

[CLICK]

Segmentation gives the first big jump. A better hierarchy leads to better geometry.

[CLICK]

Localization gives the second big jump. Sending each part to a specialist beats one single model doing everything — with the same model size.

[CLICK]

And refinement adds the final improvement in visual fidelity: render, compare, and repair.

[pause]

============================================================
27 · LIMITATIONS   (~1:15)
============================================================

There are four limitations.

First, the system currently works on single pages only.

Second, identifying the exact font family is still difficult.

Third, the refinement loop adds latency, because the page is rendered several times.

And fourth, the data leans towards high-resource scripts, like English and Chinese, and towards clean layouts.


============================================================

============================================================

FID-HTML is useful beyond reconstruction.

Document preservation.

Indexing and search.

One application I want to show in more detail is synthetic document generation.

We start from a real receipt.

[CLICK]

REPLICA converts it into FID-HTML.

[CLICK]

Because it is HTML, we can find the parts that change from one receipt to another — the store name, the address, the items, and the amounts. These are the slots, shown in gold.

[CLICK]

[CLICK]

[CLICK]

This is useful where real documents cannot be shared because of privacy — like medical records, bank statements or ID cards.

So from one real document, we can create many new ones for training.


============================================================
30 · SUPERVISION FOR GROUNDED DOCUMENT MODELS   (~1:30)
============================================================

FID-HTML is structured, but it is still text. That means REPLICA can also be used to generate training data.

[CLICK]

Every page it processes comes with text, structure, position and style — ground truth by construction.

This has already been used in three papers: M3Grounder at CVPR, and DoCoG and DocLayout-VL at ECCV.

[CLICK]

And the loop closes. Better models improve REPLICA, and REPLICA produces better training data.

[CLICK]

One example is GroundingDocQA — 200,000 documents and 2 million question–answer pairs, each grounded to the exact region on the page.


============================================================
31 · CONCLUSION — Represent → Generate → Measure   (~1:00)
============================================================

To conclude, this thesis makes three contributions.

FID-HTML: a representation in which visual fidelity is explicit.

REPLICA: an agentic framework — Segment, Localize, Assemble and Refine — using agents for judgment and tools for fixed steps.

And VFDR-Bench: a benchmark that finally measures position and visual fidelity.

[pause]

REPLICA reaches 0.93 in visual fidelity and 0.86 overall.

The main lesson is that a structured design can beat simply using a larger model.
